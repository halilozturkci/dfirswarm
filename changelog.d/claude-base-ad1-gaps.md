### Fixed: computer-forensics-base AD1: the data no item leads to is read to its end, and `ad1_extract` holds its output to a ratio

- **The data nothing led to is read to its end, under the clock.** `ad1-items` and `ad1_extract` read only the first 256 MiB of the image's data that no item, name, metadata entry, chunk table or chunk leads to, so a zeroed `next` pointer that left items behind a longer run of zeros was reported as "not read" rather than found. Every such stretch is now read in 1 MiB blocks, the clock is looked at before each block (1 GiB of sparse zeros reads in about a second), and the answer says what was read: the bytes, the non-zero bytes, the gaps that hold non-zero bytes or were not read to their end, and the first twenty of those gaps (`unreferenced_gaps` in the recipe's `coverage.json`, `unreferenced_data` in the tool's answer and coverage). Every such gap, up to 100,000, is in `unreferenced-gaps.jsonl` (recipe) or `ad1_extract.gaps.jsonl` (tool). A read the clock stops is `partial`, with the address it reached (`stopped_at`) and `integrity_status: unverified`, never `complete`. Zero bytes between structures are still padding.
- **`ad1_extract` holds what it writes to a ratio of what it read.** New `max_ratio` (default 1000, at most 1,000,000, with 1 MiB of slack, as `archive_extract` has): when the bytes written pass `max_ratio` times the compressed bytes of content read, the call stops, keeps the file it was in as a `.partial`, and says so in `stopped_by_budget` with the ratio. The 256 GiB `max_bytes` default is no longer the only bound on what a hostile image can make it write.
- **A test builds the large pads sparse** (a 64 GiB hole, 270 MiB holes, pads of 256 MiB and a block either side) without costing disk.
