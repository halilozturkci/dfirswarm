### Fixed: computer-forensics-base `disk-volumes` and `memory-windows` answer once on a stop signal, and `disk-volumes` has one clock

- **A TERM, INT or HUP ends the step running and the recipe answers once.** The two shell recipes had no `trap`: a stop ended them with no JSON, no `coverage.json` rewritten, and the step they were running (a `fsstat`, a `fls`, a Volatility plugin) left behind. Every step is now started in the background and waited for, so the signal is acted on at once; the trap ends that one step by the pid it was started with (no pattern kill, nothing else), writes `coverage.json` as `partial` with `interrupted: true` and the signal's name, prints one JSON document (`ok: false`, `status: partial`, `interrupted: true`, `signal`) and exits 128 plus the signal. A second signal during the answer changes nothing. A stop during `detect` is one document as well (`applies: false`, `interrupted: true`, exit 128 plus the signal). `memory-windows` keeps the steps that finished in `coverage.json`'s `steps`.
- **`disk-volumes` shares one clock between its steps.** Each step had 900 seconds (`RECIPE_STEP_SECONDS`) and the recipe had no total, against `recipe.json`'s `limits.seconds` of 14,400. The recipe now has `RECIPE_SECONDS` (default 14,400, as `recipe.json`; a test holds the two equal) less a margin of a twentieth, at most 60 seconds, and each step gets the smaller of its 900 seconds and what is left. A step the clock leaves no time for is not started, and `coverage.json` names it in `limits_hit` (`seconds: not started, the recipe's time limit left none for it: <the command>`); a step that was given only what was left and used it is named too. Either makes the status `partial`. A partition whose `fsstat` was not started is `failed` in the inventory with that reason, never classed as swap or reserved for a file system that was not looked for, and an image where nothing could be read before the clock ran out is `partial`, not `unsupported`.
