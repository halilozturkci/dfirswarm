### Added: triage-collection keeps the clocks of a delivery apart with `collection_timestamps`

A new tool reads two clocks as separate objects, each with its precision and its zone: an archive's member times (a zip header's DOS date and time, two-second steps with no zone and never read as UTC; a zip's extended-timestamp and NTFS fields; a tar's whole seconds; a pax mtime to the digit the writer wrote) and the delivered files' modification times (one `lstat` per entry, `modified_epoch_ns` as a decimal string). It gives each clock's earliest and latest time with the raw value, `apparent_flattening` (more than 90% of more than 20 files on one date, as a count and with no cause), a per-file comparison only where an archive member and a delivered file have the same name after NFC and case folding, and the conflicts. `local_zone` reads a zip's zoneless times under a named zone and says which times a clock change makes ambiguous or non-existent. The archive is read by the collection-intake recipe's bounded readers (the same text, held equal by a test) and never extracted: a damaged or appended gzip, bzip2 or xz stream, a tar without its end marker, a zip with bytes in front of it, or an archive inside the archive makes the answer `partial`. `verify/time-layers` names the tool. It also keeps the collector's own record times (a KAPE copy log's time columns, the timestamps of UAC's log, KAPE's console log and Velociraptor's `log.json`, a Velociraptor upload row's `Timestamp` and `started`) as the collector's raw text with the zone its writer states and the basis for it (a time with none is a wall-clock reading and is never taken to be UTC), gives each collector log's first and last timestamped line as the acquisition interval (`unknown` where no log states one, never inferred from file times), and compares a copy row's `ModifiedOnUtc` with the delivered mtime of the file the records join names, only where it names exactly one; a record cut by the clock, malformed or in another encoding makes the answer `partial`. `verify/collector-effects` names the tool too.
