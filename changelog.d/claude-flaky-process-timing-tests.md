### Fixed: the process, signal and deadline tests no longer depend on how fast the machine is

A set of tests that start a stand-in program, stop it by a deadline or a signal and ask whether it and what it started are gone failed on a loaded machine (a runner under a whole suite, a laptop with other work on it) for reasons that had nothing to do with the tools. They are tests only; no tool changes.

- Asking about the right processes: the journal_export test of a stop signal looked for `sleep 60` anywhere on the machine, so another session's sleeper failed it. The stand-ins of the journal_export, linux_triage and linux-target, Windows, network and reverse-engineering process tests now record the pid of the program they start (the parent writes it, so the child does not have to be scheduled first), and the tests ask about those pids.
- Waiting for a state, not for a time: the tests read the stand-in's pids, wait for a command line to show in `ps`, and wait for a process to be gone with a bound of 30 seconds that returns the moment it is (it was 1.5 to 3 seconds), instead of sleeping 300 ms and looking once. The sleeps that remain wait for something that must not happen (nothing is written after a group is killed).
- Deadlines that are the tool's own, long enough for a stand-in to have started: a one or two second deadline passed before a loaded machine had run the stand-in, leaving nothing to ask about. The deadlines of those tests are 5 to 8 seconds, the stand-ins sleep for 120 to 300 seconds, and the wall-clock bounds (once 6 to 30 seconds) sit at 60 to 120 seconds: far above any delay and far below the stand-in's sleep, so a tool that waited for the program still fails. The tests also ask the tool's own words, that it was ended by its deadline (`timed_out`, or "did not finish in time").
- The static-binary recipe's budget tests use a hole of 32 GiB and budgets of 20 and 120 seconds instead of 2 GiB and 4: the recipe's budget is spent by the time a loaded machine has started its first tool, and a faster pass than the budget made "partial" untrue.
- aes_inverse_scan's zeros and sparse-memory case asks the tool's counters of work (no candidate in zeros; at most five schedule checks a byte in the sparse worst case, 4.4 measured) and that the scan finished inside its own budget, instead of a 20-second wall clock.
- output-hygiene reads job.json and manifest.json once they parse: the service writes them in place just after the job reads committed, and a read between the first byte and the last failed with "Unexpected end of JSON input".
- worker-fence and stop-seal wait until `ps` shows the stand-in process under the command line the tool searches for, before they ask the tool to find it, and bound the wait for it to be stopped.
