### Fixed: the macos-forensics tools keep their clocks over the work that was outside them, and say what they could not read (macos-forensics)

A second independent verification put hostile input through the parts of the four tools that earlier rounds had not: a clock that started after the work it should bound, a missing regular-file test, a status that ignored a failed read and a clean-up that no handler covered. Each fix has a test in `tests/pack-macos-forensics-r2.test.ts` that fails on the previous tools.

- **`knowledgec_query` reads the schema under its clock and its stop signals.** The progress handler was installed after the first statement, and the first statement is where SQLite parses the whole schema: a crafted file with 200,000 to 300,000 schema rows (30 to 46 MB, `CREATE TABLE` rows written with `writable_schema`) took 142 to 150 seconds whatever `max_seconds` said, against the manifest's 120. The handler now stands right after the connection is made. When the time runs out in the schema the answer is `partial` with nothing listed and no row read, a problem names the schema, and `resume.to_go_on` says the schema is larger than `max_seconds` allows to parse; a stop signal there ends the tool at once with its one JSON answer. Measured here with 300,000 tables and `max_seconds` 10: 47.7 s before, 11.3 s after (a loaded Mac; the review measured 142 s and 150 s on its runs).
- **`fsevents_parse` holds a bounded list of problems with exact counts.** Every bad page header and junk run appended a dict to a list that was then judged one by one, outside every clock: a gzip of 12-byte headers declaring length 0 (81 KB of gzip for 40 MiB) took 253 seconds and 1.7 GB, and at the default expansion cap (256 MiB, about 0.5 MB of gzip) the tool grew past 10 GB. The first 100 problems of each kind in a file are listed one by one; the later ones are folded into one row per kind (`folded: true`, `occurrences`, `first_offset`, `last_offset`, `bytes`); at most 5,000 problem rows are listed in all and the rest are folded by kind across the files. `problem_occurrences` counts every problem, `per_file[].problems` and `coverage` hold the exact counts, and `problems_folded` says when anything was folded. Measured on 40 MiB of bad headers (3,400,000 problems): 253 s and 1.7 GB before, 3.6 s and 76 MB after, 101 problem rows.
- **`fsevents_parse` refuses a FIFO as `path`**, as JSON at once, and opens a file without waiting for a writer (`O_NONBLOCK`, then a regular-file check on the descriptor); a FIFO as `path` blocked in `open()` until the harness's kill.
- **`fsevents_parse` reads a gzip member whose header is split by the 1 MiB read**: the rest of the file was reported as "not a gzip member" (partial, the records after it lost); the tool now reads on until it has the two bytes of the header. **Members that inflate to nothing are under the clock**: it is read once in 4,096 members (12,000,000 empty members, 228 MB, ran 213 seconds of a 200-second clock and ended `complete` with 1.3 GB), and a member that produces no output is no longer listed (84 MB for the same file).
- **`plist_read` is not `complete` over a tree it could not read.** A subdirectory that cannot be listed (`walk.directories_unreadable`) and a `.plist` name in a directory that can be listed but not searched (new `walk.entries_unreadable`; it was counted as a special file) make the answer `partial`, or `failed` when nothing was read, with the places named in `status_basis` and `walk.problems`; "every one of the N matched file(s) was read" was said over both. Links that were not followed and `.plist`-named entries that are not regular files are named in the basis of a complete answer (also for the directory of `fsevents_parse`): by design they do not change the status, but they are no longer only counted. A key path with an index no list can hold (5,000 digits, a non-ASCII digit) is the file's failed row "no key", not "the tool stopped on an unexpected error".
- **`unified_log` and `knowledgec_query` finish removing their staged copy when a stop signal lands in the removal.** The `finally: rmtree` ran outside the handler that turns a signal into an answer: the answer said the tool was stopped before the reader had run, with no counts, and the copy stayed half removed (267,815 of 300,000 files in the review's runs), which also made the next run in the same `out_dir` refuse to start. The signals are held for the clean-up; a signal that lands there is named in the answer (`interrupted`, exit 128 plus the signal), which says the reader had finished and counts its output for the short time a stop leaves.
- **`knowledgec_query` removes only what it made.** The sweep of copies a killed run left (`knowledgec-<pid>-<name>` in `work/<id>`) removed any such directory with a dead process number and no lock, including one an agent had made; it now needs the shape the tool makes (mode 0700, owned by the user, only regular files of mode 0600).
- **`unified_log` bounds the preview by bytes as well as by `limit`** (new `max_inline_bytes`, default 1 MiB; an entry that does not fit is not cut, the preview ends before it and `entries_bounded_by_bytes` says so; every entry is still counted and in the output file), and refuses a FIFO or a socket as `path`.
- **The goal `mac-compromise` no longer promises TCC states the pack cannot give.** Question 4 now carries the clause question 7 has ("or state that the available parser cannot answer the question") and says the pack has no TCC parser or skill.
- Parser versions are `plist_read/5`, `fsevents_parse/5`, `knowledgec_query/5` and `unified_log/5`; the manifests say the above. The shared stop, boundary and guard blocks are untouched.
