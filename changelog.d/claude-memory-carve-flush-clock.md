### Fixed: memory-forensics mem_carve starts no periodic fsync after its clock has run out, and states what the end of a sweep costs at the cap

With `results_to`, mem_carve fsyncs the hit list every 256 MiB inside the clocked loop; once the clock had run out it still started those flushes until its next look at the clock, so one of them could carry a run past `max_seconds` by as long as the flush takes. No periodic fsync is started now once the clock has run out; what is unsynced is flushed by the final fsync, which has its margin outside the clock. A new case in `tests/pack-memory-f2-carve.test.ts` loads the tool, makes the first fsync take longer than the clock, and counts the periodic fsyncs started late: it finds many on the earlier code and none now.

The docstring and the manifest no longer say that the end of the spill path at the 16 GiB `max_results_bytes` cap was not measured. Measured on a virtual ext4 disk under WSL2 with the real paging code fed 16 GiB: the final flush, fsync and closing took 4.1 s, and a rerun over an earlier list of the same size (a byte-for-byte compare of 2 x 16 GiB read from the disk) took 51.7 s, against the 120 s between the 780 s clock and the 900 s timeout; the longest periodic fsync of 256 MiB took 0.27 s. The sweep itself wrote 40,000 hits a second there (about 7.7 GiB of hit list in 780 s), so the cap is reached only on a machine more than twice as fast. The cap is unchanged; the texts say that a disk that sustains less than about 300 MB/s of reads needs a lower `max_results_bytes`, and that each further earlier list of the same size adds a compare.
