### Fixed: memory-forensics mem_fs makes no call on a FUSE mount that can hold it past its bound

A FUSE server that stops answering holds every stat of its mount for as long as it lives, and a request it has already read cannot be interrupted, not even by SIGKILL. `mem_fs` made two such calls on its own main thread, outside any timer: the startup loop's stat of `<mount>/sys` and `os.path.ismount` in the clean-up, so a mount that did not answer could hold the tool past the 600 s of its manifest. Both are now made by a short child process that is killed at its limit: at startup the limit is the call's deadline and a mount that does not answer ends the call as not ready; in the clean-up the checks share 3 s and a mount that does not answer is named as still there and not answering. The unmount helper is no longer run with `subprocess.run`, which waits without a limit after its kill: it is killed after 12 s and waited for 2 s more, and a helper or a check that still cannot be reaped is named in the answer with its pid and left running. The answer's `cleanup_problems` lists every trouble of the clean-up, also when an earlier problem is the one named, and an interrupted answer no longer says the mount was released when the clean-up did not finish. The clean-up bound is restated in the manifest and the docstring: at most 100 s where `/proc` is read and 112 s where the process table falls back to `ps`, so 480 + 2 + 112 = 594 s stays inside the 600 s timeout. A mount that does not answer before the engine is started (a stale mount at the given path) is not covered.

A new case, `tests/pack-memory-fuse-bound.test.ts`, runs the tool against a FUSE stand-in (`tests/memory-fuse-stall.py`, a minimal server over `/dev/fuse`) whose stat never answers; it skips with a reason where `/dev/fuse` or a `fusermount` helper is missing. It hangs to its own kill on the earlier code.
