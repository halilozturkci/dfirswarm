### Fixed: the mobile pack's tools and tar recipes keep to their time limits, and no longer say `complete` over pages or members they did not read

`sqlite_freespace`, `manifest_db`, `protobuf_peek` and the tar recipes of the mobile pack (1.5.5). Each of these ran past a limit the manifest states, or said `complete` for an input it had not read to its end.

- `sqlite_freespace` with `contains` ran past the manifest's timeout: the clock of `max_seconds` did not run while the expression did, the work budget counted only a fragment's characters times the expression's cost, and a fragment of three characters is three steps of a budget of 10^9, so the budget never bound on a database of short fragments and the calls themselves (2.85 us each, against 0.12 us for the search) ran off the clock for as long as there were fragments. `max_seconds` 270 with `contains` on 66 MB of free pages of three-character fragments (4.66 million of them), as a secret job in Docker `node:22` with `--cpus=1` on a host at load 60 to 180, took 332.6 s against the 300 of the manifest. Every fragment the expression is called on now costs 1,000 steps beside its characters, and the clock skips 12 seconds at most in all, whatever the budget says, so a run ends within `max_seconds` plus 12 seconds plus the time to write the answer: the same call now takes 282.1 s (the budget is spent after about a million fragments, and the 3.67 million after them are `match` 2, counted under `contains_not_decided`; at `max_seconds` 60 the call before took 71.4 s and now 72.3 s, since what the calls take there is the 12 seconds of the allowance). What the budget leaves undecided is still a function of the sizes the answer shows. The manifest and the README said the budget was "about 7 s"; they now say that was the expression's own time (10.5 s measured on a loaded machine) and what the allowance adds.
- `sqlite_freespace` said `complete` and "every page was examined" for a database whose header had its freelist fields (bytes 32 to 39) zeroed, with 120 freed pages not read: 12 fragments of 167, from eight edited bytes. A page that is not a b-tree page is now live content only if a cell of a b-tree reached from `sqlite_master` names it as an overflow page; the b-trees are walked from the roots the schema lists (interior pages only; leaves are marked, not read), and a page nothing owns (not on the freelist the header leads to, not reached, not named as overflow) is read as free space as a listed free page is, reported as `page in no structure`, counted under `scanned.pages_in_no_structure`, and makes the status `corrupt`. On the same database the answer is `corrupt` with all 167 fragments. A stale b-tree page that the freelist no longer lists looks like a live one and used to be read as one (only its gap and freeblocks); it is reached or it is not. Live overflow pages are counted (`scanned.overflow_pages`): 300 databases of random schemas (tables, indexes, WITHOUT ROWID, auto-vacuum, every page size from 512 to 65536, rows to 200 KB) stay `complete` with no page unowned. If the schema cannot be read to tell which pages are in use, the status is `partial` and says so; beside a `-wal` or `-journal` that holds bytes, unowned pages are reported without making the status `corrupt`, since the main file is then a state between two checkpoints. `status_basis` no longer says every page was examined: it says every page was accounted for.
- `sqlite_freespace` found the runs of a region in time quadratic in its fragments (`covered_by` summed over every run accepted so far): a 64 KiB page of 8,191 fragments took 8.2 s and now takes 0.02 s, with the same runs (2,000 random regions compared with the old function: no difference).
- `manifest_db` ran `SELECT count(*) FROM Files` before its deadline loop, with no limit and no reaction to a signal: a Manifest.db of 10,752 bytes whose table b-tree points at itself hung the tool for ever and ignored SIGTERM (SQLite counts a table in one step of its virtual machine, and Python runs a signal handler only when a call returns). The count is a sum of ones, which SQLite runs row by row, under a progress handler that ends the statement at the deadline and on a stop signal; the count gets a tenth of `max_seconds`, and an answer without it says so (`files_in_manifest` null). The same guard is on every statement. The 10 KB file with `max_seconds` 2 now answers in 2.1 s (`partial`, stopped after 38,039 rows, count unknown), and SIGTERM answers with one JSON and exit 143.
- `manifest_db` held 3.3 times the largest TEXT cell (a 50 MB path was 187 MB resident, a 200 MB one 659 MB, a 1 GB cell 3.3 GB). The name columns are read to their first 8,193 characters (one past what is judged), so a longer name is the placeholder it already was and the string in Python is 8 KB; SQLite still holds the cell once while it cuts it, so memory follows the largest cell once (an 80 MB cell: 228 MB resident before, 109 MB now). Two domains over the cap stay two. The plain plists (`Manifest.plist`, `Info.plist`, `Status.plist`) are read up to 4 MiB, not 32 (three 28 MB nested-array plists were 1.1 GB resident); a larger one is said (`too_large`).
- `manifest_db` printed "withheld from every path in this answer and in the files it names" while the working copy it names beside a `-wal` holds every raw name; the note now says so.
- `manifest_db` is `partial` for every row whose metadata is not in the answer (a NULL cell, a blob that is not a keyed archive), not only `too_large` and `malformed` ones.
- `protobuf_peek` said `complete` for a window that parsed whole and ended on a field boundary with bytes after it (20 MiB of 256-byte records, the default 8 MiB window: `complete`, 12,582,912 bytes undecoded). The status is `partial` when anything lies after the window, with `window.bytes_after_window` and `window.next_offset` as before; `structure` and `consistent_with_protobuf_wire_format` stay about the window.
- The tar recipes (`ios-filesystem`, `android-backup`, and the tar watch the two LEAPP recipes share) took the first zero block for the end of the archive: ten members with the fifth header zeroed were `complete`, `tar_end: reached`, four members listed. The end is two zero blocks; a zero block with a header after it is read past, as GNU tar and bsdtar do (the library is opened with `ignore_zeros`, and the data is cut at the two end blocks), counted, and the run is `partial` with where; an archive that ends after one zero block is `tar_end: missing`; data after the end blocks (a second archive appended) is not read and is said. A file of zeros is still not a tar.
- `ios-filesystem` decoded a gzip, bzip2 or xz tar with no bound on its output (`android-backup` had one): it takes `--max-decompressed-bytes` (default 32 GiB), and a tar that expands past it stops the listing, keeps the members listed and is `partial`.
- `ios-ileapp` and `android-aleapp` ran the program with `subprocess.run`: no time limit, no process group, no handling of a stop signal, so a stop left the program running until the VM was killed. The program runs in a process group of its own with a time limit (`--max-seconds`, default 14,100 s under the recipe's 14,400); at the limit, or on SIGTERM, SIGINT or SIGHUP, the group is ended (a request, then a kill after ten seconds), what the program wrote is kept, the run says `partial` or `failed` and never `complete`, and a signal ends the recipe with exit 128 plus the signal after the program and its children are gone.

A second pass over the same pack closed what the first left, in `sqlite_freespace` above all (the pack stays 1.5.5):

- `sqlite_freespace`: the walk of the b-trees that finds which pages are in use kept every child pointer of every interior page and read the clock only in its first loop. 196 MB of fake interior pages (3,000 pages of 32,000 cells) took 44 s and 3.08 GB, and under `--memory 2g` the tool was killed with no JSON; the code before the walk answered in 0.3 s. Nothing is kept per child now: one pass sets a bit for each interior page, the walks read those pages once each (a page is marked when it is named, so a cycle or a page two parents name is counted, not followed), both walks read the clock, and more child pointers than the file has pages, which no b-tree can have, stops the walk as corruption. The same file now answers in 0.3 s (`corrupt`, "b-tree links inconsistent"), and a valid chain of 30,000 interior pages ends `partial` at the deadline.
- `sqlite_freespace`: the walk of the freelist's trunk pages had no clock and wrote one problem row per bad leaf: 3,000 trunk pages of 1,022 leaves outside the file wrote 3.06 million rows (465 MB) and ran 29.5 s at `max_seconds` 2 (about 86,000 such pages pass the manifest's 300 s). The walk reads the clock, and a kind of problem is written row by row for its first 100 rows and then as one folded row with the exact count and where the first and the last of the rest were (`problem_kinds` has the total). The same file at `max_seconds` 2: 2.1 s, no file of rows.
- `sqlite_freespace`: header bytes 52-55 (the auto-vacuum field) made pages 2, 2+span, ... "pointer maps" that were skipped without a look, so a forged flag hid a page and the answer was `complete`. A page at a pointer-map position whose entries are not pointer-map entries (a type from 1 to 5) is read as an ordinary page, reported as `pointer-map page not a pointer map`, and the status is `corrupt`: on the freelist fixture, 8 fewer fragments and `complete` before, the same fragments and `corrupt` now.
- `max_seconds` of `sqlite_freespace` and `manifest_db` is at most 255 (it was 270): the 12-second allowance, the lag of one fragment or page and the write now leave 30 seconds under the manifest's 300.
- `manifest_db`: a `Manifest.plist`, `Info.plist` or `Status.plist` over 4 MiB makes the status `partial` (it was `complete` with encryption `unknown`), and the basis of a complete answer says the completion is unknown when there is no `Status.plist`, instead of "the backup finished".
- `android-backup`: a payload of the two end blocks and no member (a valid empty archive) is `complete`, as before the lone-zero-block change; one zero block is still not an end.
- The tar watch (four recipes): a GNU long-name, long-link or pax header whose data block was wiped (its first byte is zero) is said, and the run is `partial`: the member after it carried a name the archive did not hold.
- `ios-filesystem` and `android-backup`: a clock for the listing (`--max-seconds`, default 900 and 1,500 against the recipes' 1,800), a listing that passes it is `partial` with the members listed, and a `coverage.json` that says `partial` is written before the listing starts, so a run the harness stops leaves an account.

Left open: the LEAPP recipes' group ending when the leader exits promptly on SIGTERM and a descendant ignores it (the descendant survives), `protobuf_peek`'s start-up window before its stop handlers exist (a signal in the first tens of milliseconds ends it without JSON), and `manifest_db` holding the largest TEXT cell once in SQLite and the distinct domains up to 8,193 characters each.
