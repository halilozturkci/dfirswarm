### Changed: network-forensics: the QUIC skill says what Zeek and Suricata wrote for synthetic QUIC, and the manifest says what the alignment check covers

- **What Zeek and Suricata write for QUIC was observed, once, on synthetic traffic (not a real capture).** In one throwaway container (the repository's dfirswarm base image, Debian 12) Zeek 8.0.11 (the pinned release asset, its sha256 checked against `requires/host.json`) and Suricata 7.0.10 (apt, bookworm-backports, with no rules) read 13 QUIC captures built for the purpose from RFC 9000, RFC 9001 and RFC 9369 (the key derivation was checked against the RFC 9001 test vector): a v1 and a v2 handshake with a TLS ClientHello carrying an SNI and ALPN `h3`, a Retry, a Version Negotiation, coalesced packets, a ClientHello split over two Initials, and Initials that cannot be decrypted. Nothing was captured, no network was used after the install, and the container and the downloaded file were removed. `sessions/quic-http3` now says what was seen: Zeek `quic.log` carries the version (`1`, `quicv2`, `unknown-<hex>`), the first destination id, both source ids, the SNI, the first ALPN and a history, and it also wrote `ssl.log` rows for the handshakes it decrypted (so `ssl.log` is neither a list of QUIC nor proof of its absence; the earlier sentence that it holds no QUIC was wrong for Zeek); Suricata's `quic` events carry the version, `sni`, `ja3`, `ja3s` and the extensions, and no connection ids; an Initial that does not decrypt, a Retry and a Version Negotiation show little or nothing in either. Real captures, other versions and Google QUIC (gQUIC) were not checked. The skill no longer says the pack shows nothing about Zeek or Suricata for QUIC.
- **A disclosed limit of the alignment check.** `quic_inspect` checks the lists of connection ids, versions and source ids against the packets of a datagram, not the packet type, token or frame lists; a shift in those is not flagged. The manifest and the skill say so.
