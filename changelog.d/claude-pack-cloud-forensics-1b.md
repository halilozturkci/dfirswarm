### Changed: the cloud pack's skills stop presuming failed containment, say what their tools show and do not, and keep live access out of an offline pack

The pack reads exports that were supplied; several skills read as if the examiner were at the tenant. `identity/tokens` opened with "the single most common failure in cloud incident response" and "none of those revoke what they actually hold", and ended on "a four-hour window in which the attacker still had access" computed from a reset time and a revocation time. `google/workspace` told the agent to "enumerate grants" and called Drive sharing "the exfiltration route"; its `when:` ("the tenant is Google") sent a Google Cloud case to a Workspace-only skill. `aws/cloudtrail` called `role_assumed_by` attribution, a denial burst "the first hour of an intrusion" and `StopLogging` "the equivalent of clearing the event log". `m365/unified-audit-log` said "one log for every workload", glossed `SharingSet` as anonymous sharing, a service principal's creation as a consent and `MailItemsAccessed` as a read and "the exfiltration question", and told the agent to filter by a `RecordType` the tool did not have. `entra/signins` narrowed result codes and called sign-ins "richer" than directory audit. `logs/what-exists` stated retention figures and a "thirty minutes to a day" delivery lag it could not source, and said hashing the export covers its authenticity. `aws/cloudtrail` and `m365/unified-audit-log` declared `aws` and `pwsh` although no command in either called them. All are rewritten in the defender's voice: what an artefact shows, what it does not, what else must corroborate it, every negative bounded by what was supplied, an operation or an API kept apart from its effect, a sensitive-output line wherever a tool can reach a secret (a `secret_output: true` job, never a value or a hash), and no instruction that could be read as querying a tenant or testing a recovered token.

Each skill is a leaf of at most 800 tokens (bytes / 4.245) and 300 lines, the program's bounds; the files are 20 to 28 lines and the largest is `entra/signins` at 799.5 tokens (3,394 bytes). The first line of each is "Use when ... Not for ...". What did not fit moved to second-level leaves a skill points to "only if": `logs/sources` (the source families to inventory), `google/workspace-access` (grants, delegation, sharing, forwarding) and `identity/grants` (consents, application credentials, mailbox rules as durable state). The pack carries nine skills; `requires_host:` is empty everywhere, and every front matter list matches the body. `requires/host.json` says why `pwsh` and `aws` are declared (an operator's acquisition tools; installing PowerShell installs no extractor suite; an agent never connects to a tenant with either) and `pwsh` is listed in `pack.json`'s `unreferenced_ok`. The README drops the retention figures it could not source. The five link findings of this pack leave `tests/pack-links.pending`.

`goals/tenant-compromise.md` no longer asks for "the first sign-in that was not the user" or for "what the attacker still holds" before it has been shown: it asks for the earliest sign-in the supplied logs show that the evidence supports as not the owner's (or that they do not establish one), and for revocation times as the evidence shows them or as not established, and a new check binds the revocation wording to answer 6. The five-`event` minimum is unchanged: it is a run acceptance criterion, which the owner decides.
