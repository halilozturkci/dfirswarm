### Changed: the mobile pack's skills say what each artefact shows, what it does not, and what corroborates it

The seven skills are rewritten and split into ten short leaves (the largest is `android/artifacts` at 3,358 bytes, about 791 tokens; the ten total 28,875 bytes against 16,365 before; none is over 300 lines), each opening with a "Use when ... Not for ..." line, with `needs` no more than two deep, and the detail a case needs only sometimes in a second-level leaf the first points to ("only if"): `extractions/what-you-have` and `extractions/backup-detail`, `apps/databases` and `apps/fragments`, `ios/artifacts` and `ios/containers-and-time`, `ios/biome-segb`, `ios/unified-logs`, `android/artifacts`, `location/sources`. `tests/pack-mobile-skills.test.ts` holds the budget, the opening line, an index entry of at most 40 tokens, `needs` at most two deep, the pointers to the second-level leaves, that the fields the skills name (`write_values`, `offset_verified`, `modules.tsv`, `payload_stream`, `tar_end`, `completion`, ...) are quoted fields the tools and recipes write, that the examples which run iLEAPP or ALEAPP make their output directory first (the programs refuse one that does not exist), that the goal's extraction-kind check is one awk (a `grep -q` after a pipe failed a correct report under the harness's `pipefail` when answer 1 ran to tens of kilobytes), and that the overclaims below are gone. Each says what the source shows, what it does not and what to record, bounds a negative (what was searched, in which source, over which period), dates its version facts, and carries a Sensitive output line where a tool or a store can reach message text, keys or credentials.

What they stopped saying: that an extraction's label guarantees its content (full file system, logical, "advanced logical"), that a logical extraction cannot answer a question about deletion (a database in it can hold deleted records in its own free pages and journals), that an encrypted backup is "inert: say so and stop" (the manifest of one is listed when it opens as SQLite, and which parts of one are readable depends on the build that made it), that `manifest_db` does not show when the backup was made (it prints the backup date its plists record, the computer's clock), that `manifest_db` is needed because the files are otherwise "unusable", that shared storage holds "nothing private", that every database is inventoried (discovery is by file name suffix), that a password enables a recipe that has no decryption path, that the installer field "usually decides" whether an app is the thing being looked for, that permissions are "the capability list", that `usagestats` is "the closest Android has to an execution record" (they have events and several aggregates, and `protobuf_peek` shows wire fields, not meanings), that "the newest messages are in the WAL" (a WAL may hold committed changes absent from the main file, older page versions or an unfinished transaction; a rollback journal holds pre-change pages), that fragments are recovered rows, that a routine cache row is a significant location, that EXIF is "the strongest" location source and that a photo received in a message carries the sender's coordinates, that a joined Wi-Fi name puts the phone within its range (an SSID is not a place and a saved profile is not a join), that `timestamp_decode` "settles a bare number" (it lists candidates), that "`sms.db` keeps deleted messages until it is vacuumed, which iOS does rarely", that an unresolved container makes a file uncitable, and that a Biome container timestamp is "generally the record write or harvest time" (what each timestamp means is the stream's format's to say). iOS builds are named as the evidence carries them (17, 18, 26); no schema is assumed for one that was not seen.

The skills now tell the agent what the corrected tools do: the answers of `sqlite_freespace` and `protobuf_peek` carry locators and the text only in a sealed job file (`apps/fragments` says how to read one, and what a status of partial or corrupt means), `manifest_db` gives a three-valued encryption state and reads the epoch it is told, and the LEAPP recipes' `modules.tsv` is read before any "absent". `ios/artifacts`, `ios/biome-segb` and `location/sources` name `exiftool`, `ileapp` and the tools they use in their front matter, and every skill's front matter agrees with its body. The goal template's check that answer 1 names the kind of extraction reads answer 1's own section (it matched any line of the report), and its second and fourth questions stop asking for "installers" as a finding and for a fragment without its page, offset and source.

`requires/host.json` drops `mac_apt.py` (the macOS pack declares it, and this pack's wrappers never called it) and says `idevicebackup2` is a device-backup tool that nothing in the pack runs and that does not open an encrypted backup offline; `pack.json` lists it, `heif-convert` and `pillow-heif` under `unreferenced_ok` with the reason no skill names them yet (the HEIC route has no skill in this release), and 11 lines of `tests/pack-links.pending` come out. `exiftool` is listed in `requires_host` of the two skills that name it, which resolves once the base pack declares the program: until it does, `tests/pack-links.test.sh` reports it as unresolved.
