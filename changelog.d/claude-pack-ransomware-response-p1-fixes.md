### Fixed: encrypted_survey and ransom_note_scan withhold what a withheld name carries, read their own output back, and have a clock, a stop and a status

On main, `encrypted_survey` printed the identifier-bearing directories above a note (323 of 323 canaries, every shape), the identifier shape missed whole classes, `ransom_note_scan` wrote victim identifiers into an ordinary job's output, and neither tool handled a stop signal, had a clock, or said `status`. Each fix has a test that fails on the code it replaced (`tests/pack-ransomware-names.test.ts`, `-boundary.test.ts`, `-stop.test.ts`, `-status.test.ts`, and a case in `tests/secret-output-declaration.test.ts`).

- **One rule for every printed path.** Both tools carry the same `shown()` (a block held identical by a test): every component of every path they print is judged, the directories above a note, an unreadable directory, an exclusion and the root included, and the name of a file named like a note. `encrypted_survey` prints the name of every other file as it is, in its rows, its census and its skipped and failed lists (not only an encrypted file's): it is the evidence, and an identifier in it is printed. On 300 identifier-named directories above a note (hex, UUID, alphanumeric, decimal), `encrypted_survey` printed 300 and `ransom_note_scan` 60; both print 0 now. `paths_withheld` counts a printed path once in both tools.
- **Nothing derived from a withheld name is printed.** A name that is withheld is one constant marker (before, a name caught by the token rules kept its neighbouring text and printed the length of what was cut). The appended extension of a note-named file whose name is withheld is withheld with it, counted (`appended_extensions_withheld`) and kept out of the extension table: before, 40 files named `README.txt.<16 hex>` printed 40 of 40 ids whole in the candidates, the census and the extension table, while `file` said withheld. The extension of an ordinary encrypted file is its evidence and stays.
- **The order of a listing tells nothing about a withheld name.** Both tools walked in the order of the raw names, so every table, the `sample` selection, the note ids and finding ids came out in the rank order of the withheld identifiers (12 of 12 in raw order in both tools). Directories and names are now ordered by the text printed for them, the withheld ones in an order drawn afresh every run (a keyed hash of the raw name under a salt of the run; not the inode, not the rank). In 5 runs of the 12-directory case no run comes out in raw order, and twin trees with the raw id of one withheld directory swapped give byte-identical answers. Which candidates fall in `sample`, and which note gets which id, now differ between runs when several names are withheld.
- **A second check reads every string on its way out.** Written apart from `shown()` (it reads a string whole, and calls neither the judge nor any call site's decision), it covers every row of every page and of the census and the answer, replaces an identifier-shaped string by a marker, makes a complete answer partial and says where in `boundary`. The one exception is the name of an ordinary file, which the caller declares as evidence and the check refuses for a note name. With the first rule removed (it judges nothing, scrubs nothing) no identifier reaches the answer or a file in either tool, and the two rules agree on 1,000 random names. The previous 17 call sites each decided by hand.
- **The identifier shape is wider, and its rates are written down.** A token of 8 or more letters and digits is identifier-shaped when it holds both, when it is 10 or more digits, when it is letters only and is not a word, a capitalised word, several capitalised words or all capitals, and now when it is 16 or more letters of one case (before, any run of one case was a word: 8, 12 and 24 lower-case and 8, 16 and 24 upper-case letters printed 100%, and so did base32 of 16 capitals 3.9%). Also withheld now: 8 or more pairs of hex digits joined by any character that is not a letter, a digit or a slash, wherever the run stands in a name (colon-joined 16 bytes 100% before; dash-joined 8 pairs 19.1%; see the next entry for the pairs at the edge of a name), groups of three or more digits joined by a space or a comma holding 10 digits or more (100% before), digits of any script folded to ASCII (12 Arabic-Indic digits 100% before) and the zero-width format characters (U+200B and the like) removed (hex split by them 100% before; the other invisible characters are the last entry). Measured on 1,000 random draws per shape as a directory name, printed before and after: 16 and 24 lower-case letters 100% to 0, 16 and 24 capitals 100% to 0, hex pairs joined by a colon 100% to 0, by a dash 19.1% to 0, three groups of four digits joined by a space or a comma 100% to 0, 12 Arabic-Indic digits 100% to 0, hex split by U+200B 100% to 0, base32 of 16 characters 3.9% to 0. Still printed whole, as the manifests now say: a token of 7 characters or fewer, 8 or 9 decimal digits (also of another script), groups of four joined by a dash, an underscore or a dot, two groups of 7 letters joined by a dash, 8 to 15 letters of one case, a mix of Cyrillic look-alike letters and digits (99.9%), 7 pairs of hex digits joined by a colon, a dot or a space (100%) or by a dash (29%), three groups of 3, 3 and 4 digits joined by a dash (100%), a pair glued to a letter or a digit (the run counts a pair fewer); some of the time a random 8-character letters-and-digits token (3.3%; 9 characters 2.2%, 12 characters 0.6%, 16 characters 0.1%) and capitals among small letters read as words (12 characters 5.8%, 16 characters 2.4%, 24 characters 0.5%, 40,000 draws each: the earlier "24 letters 0.03%" was not the rate of this rule). The cost, on 1,314,483 real directory components in 150,000 directories of a developer machine: 5.5% of the components and some component of 40.0% of the directories before the 16-letter rule, 5.8% and 41.8% with it (`appletvsimulator` and `managedapplications` are withheld with the 32-letter Chrome extension ids it catches).
- **Well-known directory names are printed.** `System32`, `SysWOW64`, `INetCache`, `INetCookies`, `INetHistory`, `MSOCache`, `PSReadLine`, `Framework`, `Framework64`, `SQLServer`, `GPUCache`, `DawnWebGPUCache`, `DawnGraphiteCache`, `GrShaderCache`, `IndexedDB`, `overlay2` and `datastore1` are in a short list printed in any case (before, every path under `Windows\System32` printed the marker).
- **ransom_note_scan writes values only in a secret-output job.** The manifest declares `secret_output: {"when_args": ["write_values"]}` and the tool checks `SECRET_OUTPUT=1`, as `recovery_key_scan` does: a job that sets `write_values` is sealed as secret output, and one run by hand without the variable is refused with nothing written (before, `write_values` wrote a 0600 values file into an output sealed as ordinary).
- **Stop signals, a clock, temporary files.** SIGTERM, SIGINT and SIGHUP end each tool with one JSON answer (`status: failed`, `interrupted: true`, the signal, the coverage so far) and exit code 128 plus the signal, after removing the census and pages it was writing. A values file that `ransom_note_scan` had begun is kept, cut back to its last whole row, and the answer says how many rows it holds. `max_seconds` (default 780 for the survey and 480 for the scan, at most 840 and 540, under the manifests' 900 and 600) stops the walk, writes what was counted and answers `partial`, naming the entries and directories not examined. The scan's clock now runs inside a note too: a 1,000,000-address note took 9.4 s to scan and now stops at the limit (1.1 s with `max_seconds` 1) as a note counted in `notes_partly_scanned` and carrying `scan_stopped`; reading a note past the limit is refused the same way. SIGKILL cannot be caught: it leaves the hidden temporary files of the census and pages in the output (named by no answer, holding only rows the second check has read) and, for microseconds, an empty file at a result name; the manifests and this entry say so. The job's own `timeout_seconds` is not passed to the tool: set `max_seconds` below it, or a job stopped by its timeout answers `failed`, not `partial` (in the manifests and the skill).
- **Pages are ASCII, failures say why and name no path.** A page file is written with escapes for every non-ASCII character (U+2028, U+0085 and a bidi override in a name were raw in the file, so a row could read as two lines or reorder the text), like the census. A result that cannot be written is a `failed` answer with a `status_basis` (it had none) and names the place as the answer cites it. An unexpected failure names its type and the line of the tool, never the text of the exception or the output's path (before, `no free name beside <path>`), and a refused values file is named by the path the answer cites.
- **A result file that is there is not replaced.** A rerun of `encrypted_survey` with other limits replaced the census (87,455 bytes) with a smaller one (64,960) and a candidates file (86,460) with a smaller one (63,876); every census and page is now placed without replacing: the same bytes keep their name, others take the next free `-2`, `-3`, and the answer names the file.
- **`status` and `status_basis` on every answer**, failures included; `complete` stays beside them. `encrypted_survey` is `partial` also when candidates were left unprofiled by `sample` and when an hour or extension table was capped; a head or end window is compared with its threshold unrounded (a head of 7.4997 printed as 7.5 and was a candidate).
- Parser versions are `encrypted_survey/3` and `ransom_note_scan/3`. The skills `scope/triage-tools` and `encryptor/samples-and-notes` name `status`, `status_basis`, `max_seconds` and `sample`, say that every other file's name is printed, and give the measured rates of the name rule.
- **Pairs of hex digits at the edge of a name.** The first rule counted `id[aa:bb:...:hh]`, `key=aa bb ...` and `(aa.bb. ...)` as 7 pairs (the pair next to the bracket was not a pair), and the second check needed a separator, not a slash, on both sides of the run, so 8 and 9 pairs in a directory or a file name were never seen by it (the path component is bounded by `/`). Whole 8-pair ids printed in the answer and every job file 20 of 20 for a directory name, a note name and the extension of a note-named file, in both tools, with a space, a dot or a colon (a dash 2 to 6 of 20), when the run had a bracket, `key=` or a parenthesis next to it; now 0 of 20 in every cell. A separator is now any character that is not a letter, a digit or a slash, in both rules, and only a letter or a digit glued to a pair makes it no pair. Printed before and after (1,000 draws each): 8 pairs joined by a colon, a space or a dot inside `id[ ]` or after `key=` 100% to 0, by a dash 19% to 0, 9 pairs by a dash inside brackets 10.5% to 0; the second check alone on the path form `a/<8 pairs>/b` missed 100% and now 0. A run does not cross a slash (eight two-digit directories one under another are eight names). The cost, on the 324,979 distinct names of 1,795,335 components of this machine's package and application trees: `shaped` alone (the new rule, before the shared token and key rules that judge every name too) withholds 28 more of those names, all percent-encoded non-Latin ones (`%D8%AA%D9...`, eight or more `%XX` bytes in a row), but what is printed changes for none of the 324,979: the shared rules already withheld them. The test for "the second check alone holds" now draws a table of 128 shapes (pairs of 8, 9, 12 and 16 with seven separators, in brackets and after an equals sign, in either case; hex, UUIDs, single-case letters, digits of any script, full-width forms, zero-width splits, digit groups) in five places (directory, note name, extension, root, key), and runs both tools end to end with the first rule removed.
- **The tool's own sentences are not read as names.** Any number of 10 or more digits was replaced by "a long run of digits", the default read budget (4294967296) included: a survey that spent it had `status_basis` and `partial_reasons` replaced and a `boundary` block that blamed the first rule for it, and the `reason` of a name-matched file of 10^9 bytes or more (a 5 GiB `restore_backup.vhdx`) was replaced the same way. A number of 10 or more digits followed by `bytes` is a size in a `reason`, `status_basis` or `partial_reasons`, and is printed; everywhere else, and for every other class, the check is unchanged. The type of an error (`IsADirectoryError`, `NotADirectoryError`, `BlockingIOError`; every builtin exception name) is a word, not letters that do not read as words. The place of a replaced string never names a key that came from data: a key of lower-case letters and digits was printed whole in `replaced_at` (no dict is keyed by data today; a test holds it).
- **A name withheld for a value its note holds is ordered as a withheld name.** `ransom_note_scan` ordered the walk before it read a note, so a directory named like a value the note beneath it holds, and not identifier-shaped, was withheld in the row but sat between its true neighbours in raw order (60 visible and 20 such directories: all 80 in exact raw order, 20 of 20 withheld rows between their real neighbours). A first pass now reads the notes for the values their paths hold (the same values, the same cap, a note whose path has no name of 8 characters or more is not read) before the walk that prints is ordered, at most 40% of `max_seconds`; the answer says in `first_pass` whether it finished, and when it did not every name of 8 characters or more is ordered as a withheld one. After: the 20 rows come together and in an order drawn afresh. The cost is a second read of each note whose path holds a name of 8 characters or more and a second listing: 0.62 to 1.07 CPU s on 50,000 files with a note in a fifth of the directories (see the last entry for the matching).
- **The order of names that print alike depends on the whole path.** The keyed hash covered the name only, so two directories holding the same 12 withheld names listed them in the same order in every run (rank correlation 1.00); the directory above is part of the hash now.
- **The clock is looked at between pieces of a note.** A 16 MiB note of `a.a.a.` (no match at all) took 8.9 CPU s with `max_seconds` 1 and the answer said complete, because the clock was read only when a match arrived; the matching goes through pieces of 256 KiB (a piece is searched with the longest possible match after it, so the matches are exactly the ones one search finds; a test compares them at piece sizes from 1 to 1,000), and the same run ends at 1.1 s as `partial` with the note counted in `notes_partly_scanned`. 2.4 million e-mail addresses with `write_values` and `max_seconds` 2 end at 2.2 s.
- **The receipt ends the census with the status of the answer.** A string only the answer holds (an unrecognised parameter named like an identifier) is replaced in the answer and made it `partial` while the receipt line, written first, still said `complete`; the answer is read back before the census is closed now.

Not done in this change: a name that is withheld in the rows of some notes and printed in others is ordered with the withheld ones, and a note that changed between the first pass and the second is ordered by what the first pass read; `tail_hex` prints raw tail bytes of sampled candidates (content, not a name: a footer that holds an identifier is printed in hex; the skill says to run it as a job with `secret_output: true` where key material is expected); `groups` of a value and the `length` of an identifier candidate are derived quantities (the documented pattern); a dictionary attack on the digest of a short numeric-id note; the paging block still has several texts across packs; the census holds about 0.41 KB per file with no bound; look-alike letters from another script, 8 or 9 digits, and 15 or fewer letters of one case are printed; a note value written with dashes does not withhold the digits-only name; a Cyrillic or CJK UTF-16 note without a byte-order mark is read as UTF-8. ransomware-response is re-sealed at 1.4.0.
- **A full-width solidus is not a slash, and nothing that shows nothing splits a token.** Eight or more pairs of hex digits joined by U+FF0F were printed whole by both rules in every place a name is printed, in both tools (1,000 of 1,000 draws, 20 of 20 end to end in a directory, a deep directory, a note name, an appended extension, an unreadable directory, a fifo, a link, an unreadable file, `RESTORE.pdf.id[...].locked`, `README.txt.<id>`, `readme.xlsx.<id>.ab`, `DECRYPT_<id>.docx.locked` and the root): NFKC turns U+FF0F into `/`, and a run does not cross a slash. The signs `a/c`, `a/s`, `c/o` and `c/u` did the same. All five code points are now one separator that is not a slash before the name is folded, in both rules (after: 0 of 1,000, and 0 of 1,000 with the first rule removed, for each of the five; every code point whose NFKC form holds a slash is held by a test). The format characters were removed from a name, other characters that show nothing were not: a combining grapheme joiner, variation selectors, Mongolian free variation selectors, hangul and khmer fillers, a braille blank, a combining acute accent split hex into 4s, 12 digits into 3s and 12 letters-and-digits into 6s, and all three were printed whole (300 of 300 draws, 20 of 20 end to end, the manifests' "hex split by zero-width characters 0 of 1,000" was measured with U+200B only). Format characters, combining marks (taken off the character they sit on before it is composed with it), variation selectors, fillers and the other default-ignorable code points are now removed in both rules: 0 of 7,875 draws per shape and tool (every such code point, in the middle and at either end of the token).
- **The first pass is not a second scan of every note.** The clock test of the scan failed on both Linux node jobs: the first pass (at most 40% of `max_seconds`) matched every pattern over the whole note again, so on a slow CPU (a container with 0.35 CPUs, a 7 MB note, `max_seconds` 1) the main pass had no time left to reach the e-mail pattern. A note is now matched in the first pass only when it holds, case-folded, one of the 8-character pieces of the names on its path (a value of 8 characters or more inside a name begins with one; up to 64 pieces are looked for, then the note is matched as before), and URLs, which hold a slash and so are in no name, are no longer one of the values kept for withholding a name, in either pass (a note of 5,100 URLs and a labelled identifier kept 5,000 URLs and not the identifier; the directory named like it was printed). A note is also matched for onion addresses, e-mail addresses and ethereum addresses only when it holds the literal such a match must hold (`.onion`, `@`, `0x`; a search for the literal, not a pass of the pattern: the onion pattern took 0.29 CPU s over a 7 MB note with none, now 0.007), in both passes. Six 12 MiB notes with no match: 21.6 CPU s before this entry, 7.4 after (11.4 with no first pass at all); 50,000 files with a note in a fifth of the directories (2 KB notes): 2.1 and 1.6 to 2.0 CPU s (1.3 with no first pass). In a container with 0.35 CPUs the 7 MB note of e-mail addresses with `max_seconds` 1 counted none of them (3 of 3 runs; 0 to 4,636 before the first pass existed), and now counts about 45,000, and 206,000 against 109,000 with one CPU. The clock test asserts the partial status and the clock stop, not a count that depends on the speed of the CPU.
- **The clock is looked at inside the URL and identifier patterns too.** Both had no longest match and were run in one call: a 16 MiB note of `identifier`, 64 spaces and `!` took 4.98 CPU s with no look at the clock, in each pass (`max_seconds` 2: 6.2 s of wall time; now 2.2 s). Their matches are now found piece by piece with enough text after each piece for the part before the open end (a match that reaches the end of what was searched is matched again from its start with all the text); 800 random texts at piece sizes 7 to 300 give exactly the matches of one search.
- **What the shape rule does not cover is written down as a class.** The rule is a deny-list of shapes, and the shapes are not finite: each review found more that print 100%, and the manifests and the skill now say so, with the classes found and their rates (pairs joined by two characters or more, 7 pairs, MAC and IPv6 addresses, other groupings of digits and letters, CJK, emoji, Greek look-alikes, words joined by a dash, pairs across directories, a pair with a letter or a digit glued to it: 100% by a colon, a space or a dot and 3 to 14% by a dash, not "whatever stands at either end of the run"). The pull request recommends an allow-list design for the owner to decide.
