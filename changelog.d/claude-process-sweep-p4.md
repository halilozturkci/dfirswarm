### Fixed: the base pack's tools no longer answer complete over a file that a program, or a process the program left behind, may still write; the disk-timeline recipe ends such a process itself

- **archive_extract, feature_scan, image_verify, layer_describe and timeline_super call `process_apply`, or put the verdict into their own status.** The shared block already ended a process that a program left behind (a child of 7z, bulk_extractor, ewfverify, mmls or psort that held a file, in the tool's group or after setsid, or opened it by name later) before the file was counted, hashed or read, but the answer still said `complete` over a file cut at the moment the process was ended. It is now `partial`, says how many processes were ended (`program_group.left_behind`) and why (`status_basis`, and `problems` or `partial_reasons`), and an answer that reaches the end of a run carries `program_group` (a refusal, or a failure before a program ran, does not). The receipt of `image_verify` and `archive_extract`, the descriptor of `layer_describe` (it is named by its own hash, so it holds the status) and the exit code of `feature_scan` and `timeline_super` follow the answer. `browser_storage` does the same when `write_values` is asked for, because the reader writes the values file; without `write_values` it only reads what the reader printed on a pipe, and a process that was ended still makes it `partial`.
- **Where the absence of a leftover cannot be proven, a tool that reports a file a program wrote is at most `partial`.** On a host that is not Linux, or where the kernel refuses `PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER`, the five tools above, `browser_storage` with `write_values` and the disk-timeline recipe answer at most `partial` and say why (`program_group.unproven`). `archive_extract` on a zip, a tar or a gzip, bzip2 or xz file runs no program and is not held to it; a zstd stream read through the `zstd` program, and `browser_storage` without `write_values`, print on a pipe and are not held to it either (a process that was ended still makes them `partial`).
- **timeline_super's `--version` probes run under the shared block.** They were `subprocess.run(timeout=)`, which ends only the probe itself; a probe that does not end is now killed with what it started.
- **The disk-timeline recipe ends what log2timeline, psort or pinfo leave behind with a mechanism of its own.** It carries no shared PROCESS block (adding it would change the census), so on Linux it makes itself a subreaper, ends what a step left (stopped first, then killed and reaped, for at most 3 seconds) before it reads the logs, counts the CSV or lists the files, and the run is `partial` with `program_group` in `coverage.json`. A stop signal now ends everything the step started and lost, not only the step's own process.
- **A program that ends while something it started keeps its output pipe open no longer holds a pipe reader.** `image_layout` and `file_type` (their `communicate()` after a timeout had no clock, so a leftover `sleep` on the pipe held them past the manifest's limit), `archive_extract` on a zstd stream (a blocking read), `icat_extract`, `chunk_needles` and the archive-members recipe now read on their own clock, give the pipes one second after the program has ended, then let them go and end what the program left behind (a subreaper on Linux, as the disk-timeline recipe does). `image_layout` and `file_type` count the program as not finished and take none of its output; `archive_extract` takes the zstd stream as damaged; `icat_extract`, `chunk_needles` and the recipe answer `partial` and say the end of the stream was not seen, where they waited for `max_seconds` (or the recipe's limit) before.
- Pack raised: `computer-forensics-base` 1.16.2 over the batch (archive_extract 18, browser_storage 3, feature_scan 16, image_verify 4, layer_describe 4, timeline_super 17, image_layout 14, file_type 14, icat_extract and chunk_needles one higher, disk-timeline 1.1.10, archive-members one patch higher). Still open: the log, stdout and stderr files hold what a leftover wrote before it was ended (the answer says so); a writer that is no descendant of the program (started through a service manager) is found by none of these; the pipe readers end a leftover only where the kernel lets the tool be a subreaper, and `chunk_needles`, `icat_extract`, `image_layout`, `file_type` and the recipe do not hold a non-Linux host to the rule.
