### Fixed: the base pack's tools no longer answer complete over a file that a program, or a process the program left behind, may still write; the disk-timeline recipe ends such a process itself

- **archive_extract, feature_scan, image_verify, layer_describe and timeline_super call `process_apply`, or put the verdict into their own status.** The shared block already ended a process that a program left behind (a child of 7z, bulk_extractor, ewfverify, mmls or psort that held a file, in the tool's group or after setsid, or opened it by name later) before the file was counted, hashed or read, but the answer still said `complete` over a file cut at the moment the process was ended. It is now `partial`, says how many processes were ended (`program_group.left_behind`) and why (`status_basis`, and `problems` or `partial_reasons`), and every answer carries `program_group`. The receipt of `image_verify` and `archive_extract`, the descriptor of `layer_describe` (it is named by its own hash, so it holds the status) and the exit code of `feature_scan` and `timeline_super` follow the answer. `browser_storage` does the same when `write_values` is asked for, because the reader writes the values file; without `write_values` it only reads what the reader printed on a pipe, and a process that was ended still makes it `partial`.
- **Where the absence of a leftover cannot be proven, a tool that reports a file a program wrote is at most `partial`.** On a host that is not Linux, or where the kernel refuses `PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER`, the five tools above, `browser_storage` with `write_values` and the disk-timeline recipe answer at most `partial` and say why (`program_group.unproven`). `archive_extract` on a zip, a tar or a gzip, bzip2 or xz file runs no program and is not held to it; a zstd stream read through the `zstd` program, and `browser_storage` without `write_values`, print on a pipe and are not held to it either (a process that was ended still makes them `partial`).
- **timeline_super's `--version` probes run under the shared block.** They were `subprocess.run(timeout=)`, which ends only the probe itself; a probe that does not end is now killed with what it started.
- **The disk-timeline recipe ends what log2timeline, psort or pinfo leave behind with a mechanism of its own.** It carries no shared PROCESS block (adding it would change the census), so on Linux it makes itself a subreaper, ends what a step left (stopped first, then killed and reaped, for at most 3 seconds) before it reads the logs, counts the CSV or lists the files, and the run is `partial` with `program_group` in `coverage.json`. A stop signal now ends everything the step started and lost, not only the step's own process.
- Pack raised: `computer-forensics-base` 1.16.2 over the batch (archive_extract 18, browser_storage 3, feature_scan 16, image_verify 4, layer_describe 4, timeline_super 17, disk-timeline 1.1.10). Not changed, and listed: `chunk_needles`, `icat_extract`, `file_type`, `image_layout` and the archive-members recipe start a program with their own `Popen` and read its pipe (and, for `icat_extract`, a stderr file that holds only a message); a child that keeps the pipe open holds them to their own deadline, and a stop kills only the direct child. Still open: the log, stdout and stderr files hold what a leftover wrote before it was ended (the answer says so); a writer that is no descendant of the program (started through a service manager) is found by none of these.
