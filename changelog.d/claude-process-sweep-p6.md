### Fixed: the mobile, linux and memory tools that run a program no longer answer complete over what a process the program left behind may have written, and the readers of a pipe no longer wait for a process that holds it

The shared PROCESS block already ends what a program leaves behind (`settle()`) before a tool sizes, hashes or reads what the program wrote; these tools now say so in the answer. A tool whose answer would be `complete` is `partial` when `settle()` ended a leftover (the files are stable, but they may be short: what the process would still have written is missing) and a line says so under `problems`, `errors` or the status basis.

- **Tools that report a file a program wrote** (`heic_inspect`: exiftool's output and the preview; `backup_decrypt`: the decrypted files, written by the tool's own child; the `android-aleapp`, `android-backup-apps` and `ios-ileapp` recipes: the reports, the logs and the files kept; `linux_triage` and the `linux-target` recipe: dissect.target's family files; `journal_export`: the export and journalctl's stderr file; `vol_views` with `dump`: the exported files) are also at most `partial` on a host that is not Linux, or where the kernel refuses `PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER`, because there the absence of a leftover cannot be proven (the owner's rule). `linux_triage` records `left_behind` in the entry of the function whose program left the process; the `linux-target` recipe composes the wrapper's verdict (its summary carries `program_group`) with its own, and `coverage.json` carries both.
- **Tools that report only what a program printed on a pipe** (`package_verify`: debsums or rpm; `audit_events`: ausearch, the second reader; `vol_readiness`, `mem_registry`, `vol_views` without `dump`, `isf_intake`: Volatility and dwarf2json) are `partial` for a process `settle()` ended, because what it wrote into the pipe cannot be told from the program's output, and carry the verdict, but a host that cannot adopt orphans does not make them `partial` (`program_group.unproven` says why). `isf_intake` keeps no table when its converter left a process: the table is removed, the verdict is `unknown` and the status `partial`.
- **Which answers carry `program_group`** (`subreaper`, `left_behind`, `unproven`, `notes`): every answer of these tools that ran a program (the final answer; `heic_inspect`, `backup_decrypt`, `journal_export` in both modes, `package_verify`, `audit_events`, `linux_triage` in every summary it writes, `vol_views`, `vol_readiness`, `mem_registry`, `isf_intake`; `coverage.json` and the printed line of the recipes). A refusal, an early failure before a program ran, a `failed` answer that `fail()` prints and the `detect` step of the `linux-target` recipe carry none.
- **The readers of a pipe are bounded.** `journal_export` (journalctl's stdout), `package_verify` (debsums or rpm) and `audit_events` (ausearch) counted the pipe grace only from a quiet moment: a leftover that held the pipe and wrote a byte every 200 ms held the tool to its own clock (`journal_export` and `package_verify`: `max_seconds`, up to 270 and 240 seconds; `audit_events`: the second reader's 10 or 30 seconds), and `journal_export` also waited for a quiet holder (no byte at all) for `max_seconds`: its loop never looked at whether journalctl had ended. The grace now runs from the program's end whether bytes arrive or not, a holder is ended by `settle()` and the pipe is let go a second later (after the program ended: a holder of the pipe costs the tool about three seconds, not the program's clock). The `linux-target` recipe read its wrapper with an unbounded `communicate()`; it now reads under a clock of 14300 seconds (under the 14400 of its limit), a process that holds the wrapper's pipes is let go after a second, and a wrapper that is still running at the clock is ended with everything it started (coverage `errors` says so). The run_engine readers of the four memory tools, the block's `communicate_bounded` (the version probes, the recipe's detect step) and `mem_fs`'s drain threads were probed with a sleeper, a slow writer, a holder of the standard input, a 1 ms writer and a `yes` flood, on the merged head with the cap of the shared block (P0b): a holder or a flood that starts after the program ended is read for at most a second and 1 MiB by `communicate_bounded` (the version probes, the recipe's detect step), ended, and counted (a cut makes a `process_apply` carrier `partial`); the measured answer times of the holder tests are at most about 5 seconds (the tests allow 10). A flood that starts while the program is still alive is not capped by that block: the readers of these tools stop at their own byte limits (for `audit_events` the second reader's 32 MiB). A slow writer that adds bytes to the name target-query printed makes the `detect` step answer `unknown` (exit 2), which closes no route.
- **A leftover that floods a pipe is counted (first verifier round).** The four memory tools closed the engine's pipes in `run_engine`'s `finally` and only afterwards left ACTIVE, which is when `settle()` runs: a leftover writing at full speed (`yes`, a 1 ms loop) died of SIGPIPE at the close, `left_behind` stayed 0 and the answer was `complete` over its lines (counted as rows, or appended to the `isf_intake` table). `run_engine` now looks (`reap_orphans` on the pipe inodes, then `settle()`) before the pipes are closed; the answer is `partial`, `isf_intake` keeps no table, and the status basis says the rows, counts or table of that run are not to be trusted (the lines the leftover wrote before it was ended cannot be told from the program's, and are not cut out). The tests now also use a 1 ms writer, a `yes` flood and a stderr flood, for all four tools and for the dump path. The recipes' programs write to files, so their holder tests leave the floods out (a flood into the program's own stdout file is read at the log parser's speed). `journal_export`, `package_verify` and `audit_events` call `settle()` before they close their pipes on the exit path; their cap and deadline paths close first (`audit_events` now counts a leftover there, see the next bullet). The `linux-target` recipe's stop handler now also ends what the wrapper left after it exited and prints its JSON line (it exited 129/143 with none and left the holder alive). The memory PARSER labels follow the manifest versions (4).
- Sentences that depend on P0b (the shared-block cap on floods) were re-read against the merged head and measured: the post-exit cut at 1 MiB or 1 second, the counted cut (`PIPE_CUT`) that makes a `process_apply` carrier `partial`, and the answer times above. `audit_events` now also counts (`settle()`) a flooding holder it finds when its own cap or clock ends the read after the program had ended; before, it ended it uncounted and answered `complete` (found by the holder matrix on the merged head). The version probes of `package_verify` and `audit_events` and the detect step are tested with every holder, floods included (they were not before).
- Packs raised (on the batch head 5e1b3b71): `mobile-forensics` 1.10.4 (heic_inspect 5, backup_decrypt 4, android-aleapp 1.1.6, android-backup-apps 1.1.4, ios-ileapp 1.2.3), `linux-forensics` 1.12.5 (linux_triage 14, journal_export 19, package_verify 4, audit_events 5, the linux-target recipe 1.1.13), `memory-forensics` 1.15.7 (vol_views 5, vol_readiness 5, isf_intake 5, mem_registry 5). Read and left as they were: `ab_layout`, `backup_materialise`, `manifest_db`, `protobuf_peek`, `sqlite_freespace`, the `android-backup` and `ios-filesystem` recipes, `auth_log`, `container_inventory`, `container_layers`, `cron_dump`, `linux_persistence`, `shell_history`, `utmp_parse`, `carve_handoff`, `mem_carve`, `mem_profile` (they start no program), and `mem_fs` (no PROCESS block: its engine is a FUSE server that it stops and unmounts itself, its files are copies it makes from the mount, and a holder of the engine's pipes is ended by its own search and named in `clean_up`).
- Still open (listed, not chased): the exported or kept files hold what a leftover wrote before it was ended (the answer says so); a writer that is no descendant of the program (started through a service manager) is found by nothing here; a `-h`, `--version` or `--help` call that leaves a process counts in the tool's `left_behind` and makes the answer `partial`, though no file of the run is affected; `backup_decrypt` is held to the non-Linux rule although its child is this repository's own Python (the library is not); `mem_fs` does not find an engine descendant that closed its copies of the pipes and left the engine's group (it names what it ends).
