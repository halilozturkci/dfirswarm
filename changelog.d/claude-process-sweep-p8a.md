### Fixed: the small findings the process-sweep verifiers listed as P3 (a tool that crashed on an edited export manifest, flood costs in two readers, a stop that left orphans, and sentences that were not true)

- **`esedb_query` (`windows-forensics` 1.7.31, tool 20; the `tool-library` twin is byte-identical).** `reusable()` only checked that the `program_group` of an export manifest was a dict, so an edited manifest such as `"program_group":{"left_behind":"x","unproven":5}` made `export_doubt()` raise a TypeError and the tool print no JSON. The whole shape is checked now (`left_behind` a whole number of 0 or more, `unproven` and `notes` lists of strings); a manifest with any other shape is treated like one from an older version, and the export is made again.
- **`tcrypt_header` (tool 10) and `crypto_metadata` (tool 6), `encrypted-containers` 1.14.6.** `tcrypt_header` parsed the whole 1 MiB the shared cap keeps of cryptsetup's output line by line (3.5 to 6.4 seconds of CPU on a `setsid` `yes` leftover; the 1.3 seconds at 46 MB in the P5 fragment hold for short-line floods only). It parses the first 64 KiB, cut at a line end, and judges each field once; a real `tcryptDump` block is a few hundred bytes and is read exactly as before. `crypto_metadata` read what a reader wrote to its file, up to the 8 MiB cap, through the LUKS1 and BitLocker patterns line by line (17.9 seconds on 6 MB of empty lines); both parsers read the first 64 KiB. Probed and left alone: `archive_metadata` and `archive_probe` (a `yes` leftover is read for the one-second pipe grace, 1.2 seconds of CPU, which is that clock and not a parse cost), `crypto_selftest` and `password_recover` (the version-line parsers cost 0.01 second on 1 MiB); the `fvdeinfo` and `fsapfsinfo` parser costs 0.1 second on 1 MiB and is not capped. The P5 sentence "`unsupported` and `unavailable` carry none" was not true of `archive_metadata` and `archive_probe`, whose `unavailable` answer carries `program_group`; it says so now.
- **`unified_log` (`macos-forensics` 1.9.4, tool 13).** `end_leftovers_on_stop()` was defined and never called, so a stop signal that landed while the tool's own sweep was still ending orphans left the ones it had not yet reached running, writing after the answer. The stop path of `run_log` calls it (signals held, errors swallowed). With 400 `setsid` sleepers and SIGTERM half a second into the sweep, 353 were still running after the answer; none are now.
- **The `linux-target` recipe (`linux-forensics` 1.12.6, recipe 1.1.14).** The line the stop handler prints said `partial` and "coverage.json says partial" even when the wrapper had left no summary and the `coverage.json` it wrote said `failed`. The line now carries the status `coverage.json` carries.
- **Sentences corrected, no behaviour changed.** `claude-process-sweep-p0` said no carrier calls `process_apply` (true of that change alone; it now points to the fragments of the merged batch); `claude-process-sweep-p6` gave the memory parser labels as following the manifest versions "(4)" (they are 5); `claude-process-sweep-p1` and `claude-process-sweep-p3` said every answer carries `program_group` (a refusal and a failure before a program ran carry none), and the p3 fragment names the `network-capture` recipe's `tshark -G fields` read as a reader bounded only by its own clock.
- **Test only.** The unbound-name scan in `tests/tool-library.test.ts` (item 33) now knows the module attributes (`__file__`, `__name__`, `__doc__`, `__package__`, `__spec__`, `__loader__`) and has a negative case, so it no longer reports `__file__` in the `ransomware-response` and `virtualization-backup-forensics` tools.
