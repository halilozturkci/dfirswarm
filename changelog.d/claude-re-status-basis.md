### Fixed: fuzzy_hash and the capa-floss recipe carry a status_basis on every answer

`fuzzy_hash` answered with a `status` and no reason for it on any path, and the capa-floss recipe's `coverage.json` and its printed answer did the same; a reader had to work out from the problems which engine or which budget had made an answer partial. Every `fuzzy_hash` answer now carries a one-sentence `status_basis`: complete says that ssdeep and tlsh each ran on every file within the call's 270-second budget and answered (a digest of the engine's own shape, or its own `insufficient_input`); partial gives the number of problems and the first of them, which names the engine, its status (`timeout`, `unrecognised_output`, `launch_failed`, `not_attempted`) and the file; every refusal (a missing or special file, a bad argument, a program that is not installed) and the last word of a stop by signal carry one too. `coverage.json` of the recipe carries `status_basis`, and so does the recipe's printed answer (the same sentence): complete says every engine ran, exited 0 and printed output of the shape the recipe reads; otherwise it names each engine that did not complete, with its status and reason, and any stop of the recipe itself (a signal, an unexpected error). A format the recipe does not run on (`unsupported`) and every refusal (usage, no target, a target that is not a readable file, no `--out`, a bad `--seconds`, an output directory that cannot be written) are now answered with `status: failed` and a `status_basis`, not only an `error`. `fuzzy_hash` is version 5 and the recipe 1.0.2; the shared process block of both is unchanged. Pack `reverse-engineering`.
