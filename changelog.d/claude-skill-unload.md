### Added: a skill body a seat has finished with leaves its context, and the compaction's summary is written from ids and sizes

Stacked on the skill-delivery change (index in the prompt, `skill_done`, the ledger). Over 67 recorded runs, 71 % of the 82 skill bodies seats fetched were never named or used again, and 114 of the 120 fetches a compaction followed were summarised away with none loaded again; Pi has no unload, and its compaction cut each tool result to 2,000 characters before writing the summary. What changed:

- **The unloader.** A body the seat marked done with `skill_done` is replaced in the seat's context by a one-line stub, `<id> released (N tokens). Re-load with skill('<id>').` It is a `context_edit` draft the swarm extension returns from Pi's `turn_end` (Pi 0.87.1 appends a boundary's drafts to the session before it builds the next request from the session's projection), so the next model call is sent the stub. The session keeps the raw tool result and the edit beside it as an append-only line: custody hashes what it always hashed, and the replay and cost readers read the same entries. `skill(id)` after a release delivers the body again and the `skill` row says `reload_after_release`; `skill_done` on a released note says so. The `skill_unload` trace row carries the note, its sha256 and tokens, the tool call whose result was replaced, the reason (`done` or `compaction`), the turn, how many bodies left in the boundary, and `suffix_tokens`, an estimate of what a provider's cache writes again.
- **`--skill-release auto|compaction|off`** (`SWARM_SKILL_RELEASE` in the seats; recorded as `skill_release`; said at kickoff; refused if it is none of the three). `auto`, the default: at the next turn boundary on a model whose history may be edited, and only at a compaction on a model whose catalogue entry reasons over a signing API with thinking on. The class is read from Pi's catalogue entry for the seat's model (`api`, `reasoning`) and its thinking level, not from the model's name; a test walks the whole catalogue. Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5 (Pi 0.87.1's catalogue has no 5.5; and every other reasoning Claude entry) are in the second class, because Anthropic says a client-side edit of an earlier turn can invalidate the thinking blocks of every later one; nobody has checked against a real Claude session whether editing one earlier tool result does. `compaction`: only at a compaction on every model. `off`: never, and nothing is shaped. A `skill_release_policy` row says what a seat's policy came to, and again whenever its model or thinking level changes.
- **The rules.** Only a body the agent marked done is released; the notes it is working from are never touched, however many it holds. A body no assistant turn has read waits one boundary. All the finished bodies of a boundary go in one return (one rewrite of the history). A body released once between two compactions is not released again when the agent loads it again: no toggling. When a seat has saved its hand-off note, the finished bodies are replaced in that boundary, just before the compaction rewrites the prefix anyway. The cost is in `docs/packs.md`: a mid-run release is not a saving on a cache-priced provider; it is for the model's attention.
- **The compaction is told ids and sizes.** The summary call is given every skill body as one line naming the note and its size (a released one is already a stub) and a `<skills-read>` block: what was read, what the seat marked done, what it did not (probably still needed). `prompts/compaction-summary.md` asks for those under Critical Context. The hand-off header lists the notes read since the last compaction with their sizes and which are probably still needed, never their text. The ledger rebuild at a compaction and at start knows a result that a `context_edit` replaced is not held.
- **Measured.** The context audit and the Packs tab count, per seat, the bodies released, the tokens that took out of the context, the share released at a compaction, and the loads that came after a release (the wasted-release rate). The worker prompt's Skills section says a released body is a one-line stub and `skill(id)` brings it back.

Not done here: releasing at Pi's own threshold or overflow compaction (no boundary comes before it), a staleness release of bodies the agent never marked done, a suffix-size guard on cache-priced providers, the `--skill-release` field in the console's kickoff form. Not verified: the Anthropic thinking-block behaviour above, and what the Codex OAuth endpoint does with a changed prefix.
