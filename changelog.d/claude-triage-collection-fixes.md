### Fixed: triage-collection says partial where it could not tell, and prints no digest of a credential store anywhere

- **`collection-intake` no longer reads a cut tar as a finished one.** A plain tar that ends where a member header should start, or part-way into one, used to be `complete` with the claims of the missing members `missing`. A tar now has to end with its end-of-archive marker and nothing after it: without the marker the run is `partial`, the claims it cannot find are `unverifiable` (the census is not complete), and the status basis says where the stream ended; bytes that are not zero after the marker (a second archive appended to the first) make it `partial` too. A gzip, bzip2 or xz stream that does not reach its end (the tar inside can be whole while the stream around it is cut), a gzip trailer that is cut or whose length is not the bytes returned, and a second gzip member after the first are `partial` too. `detect` carries the same note (`listing_note`) and still applies when a record is named.
- **An archive inside the archive that may hold records makes the run `partial`, and it is named** in the status basis and in `nested_archives_not_opened_and_not_collected_files`. A nested archive that is a file the collector copied from the source host (below `[root]` or `uploads`, or named by a record as a delivered file) is still named in `nested_archives` and does not.
- **The recipe has a clock inside a member.** `detect` read a tar's member data without looking at the clock (a 2 GiB zero member took 6.8 s, so a 90 MB `.tar.gz` could outlast the 300 s window); every read of the archive now checks it and the listing ends as "could not tell". The same clock stops a `run` between two reads of a member.
- **The records kept on disk at once are bounded.** A record named like a collector's is kept, whole, for parsing; the total is now at most 1 GiB (`COLLECTION_INTAKE_KEPT_TOTAL_BYTES` sets another), a record that would pass it is not kept or parsed and is named in the status basis and in `records_not_kept_total_cap`, and a record the readers refuse as binary (UTF-16 or NUL bytes in its first 4 KiB) is not kept at all and is reported `unsupported` for the same reason. A 7 MB zip of six 256 MiB `uac.log` members held 1.50 GiB at once; it holds nothing now, and 1.00 GiB at most when the members are text.
- **`collection_coverage` claims no absence from an incomplete census.** `requested` said `recorded_not_delivered` for a target whose directory could not be listed; it is now `recorded_delivery_not_decided` (records say copied, the census cannot decide), `unknown_census_incomplete` or `unknown_records_unread` where a negative would have been said, and `neither_delivered_nor_recorded` / `recorded_not_delivered` only from a complete census and records read whole. A question's statement no longer says "The delivery holds nothing under these patterns" over an incomplete census: it says what was not examined (counts, no path), the state is `census_incomplete`, and `no_file_and_no_record` is kept for a complete census.
- **The digests a credential store carries are withheld in every output.** `collection_manifest_parse` and the recipe's `records.jsonl` kept the `sha256`, `md5` and `sha1` of a Velociraptor upload record whole in `fields`; a record that names a credential store now has every digest-named string in its `fields` (at any depth) replaced by the withheld marker, and the real value goes to the values file of a job run for secrets. Another file's digests stay. Verify, path map and coverage already printed none.
- **A file an ambiguous record may name is neither `unexpected` nor "named by no record".** Verify counts it apart (`delivered_files_an_ambiguous_record_may_name`), `collection_path_map` gives it the status `candidate_of_ambiguous_record` with the records that may name it, and `collection_coverage` leaves it out of `delivered_without_a_record`; the three agree.
- The coverage skill and the tool manifests say what the tools do: a negative is stated only from a complete census, and the `requested` states are listed. Each fix has a test that fails on the code it replaced.
