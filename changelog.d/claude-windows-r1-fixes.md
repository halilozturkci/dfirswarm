### Fixed: registry_recover and lnk_jumplist no longer spend time and memory out of proportion to a small hostile file, and no longer call a damaged one complete

`registry_recover` scans a log's entries within the tool's own clock and bounds. An entry keeps the number and the place of its page references, not the list, and the clock is looked at while the log is scanned, while the pages hash is checked and while the pages are written; a replay that runs out of time leaves no recovered copy, lists the entries it would have applied as not applied and says so. An entry whose hashes hold is still not applied when it lists no page, declares a hive bins size that is not a positive multiple of 4096 or is more than the hive file's own bins plus 64 MiB, names a page that is not whole 512-byte sectors or lists more pages than its bins have sectors: the reason is under `unrecovered`, and the scan stops there and names the rest of the log (where it starts, how many bytes) instead of reading it. At most 50,000 entries of a log are scanned; a long list of page references is listed as hive ranges up to 20,000 per entry and the rest is named by its place in the log. A 16-million-reference entry that took 465 s and 2.4 GB, and a 512 MB log of a million empty entries that took 78 s and 1.4 GB, are refused in the first block. A hive bins size of 2 GiB or 4 GiB no longer produces a recovered copy of that size (the 4 GiB one was an out-of-memory kill with no JSON); a hive or copy over 512 MiB is replayed and not read, and `records.status` says `skipped` with the reason. The recovered copy now carries what the specification has the log provide for the base block: the root cell offset and the last-written time from the base block of the log that holds the last applied entry, and the flags of that entry; a log whose own base block checksum is wrong is not believed for them and the status is partial. A key name, a value name or a value data size longer than the cell it was read from is no longer followed into the next cell, where a withheld value's bytes could end up in a path or a name (126 of 1,000 damaged hives did): the record's name says it was withheld and where, a value under such a name is withheld, a data size is cut at its cell, and each is a problem line that makes the status partial.

`lnk_jumplist` reads a compound file with each sector held by one chain, so a second chain that enters it stops there and says so, and the work is linear in the sectors of the file. A 1 MB file whose DIFAT lists one FAT sector again and again (1.1 GB), and a 2 MB file with 400 streams on one 4,000-sector chain (6.2 s and 694 MB although `max_seconds` was 10), are now read in 0.1 s at about 35 MB; the clock is looked at while the FAT, the chains and the streams are read. The container is checked, not only read: the header, the DIFAT, the FAT marks of the FAT and DIFAT sectors, directory name lengths and object types, a tree that loops or names an unallocated entry or one twice, allocated entries the tree never reaches, a stream with a child, the mini stream's declared size, chains that are longer or shorter than the size they serve, and mini sectors shared by two streams. A DestList entry that names a stream that is no link, two entries that name one stream, and a container with streams and no DestList are container problems (a container with no stream at all, which is what Windows leaves for an empty jump list, is not). Of 1,000 damaged jump lists, 37 were called complete although olefile reports a defect and 11 were complete without a DestList; none now are, and 55 jump lists from real collections read as before. A problem that repeats is one line with its count.

The recipe `windows-artefacts` no longer follows a log beside a hive that is a symbolic link (a dangling one failed the whole hive) or hands it to `registry_recover`: the hive is read without it, the row names it, and the hive's call ends partial at best; a legacy single `.LOG` is passed as the first log when there is no pair. When it is stopped by a signal, the family being read is `interrupted`, every family not yet started is `not_attempted` with its inputs and calls counted, and the status text no longer claims the families listed are what had been read. The skills `registry/overview`, `artifacts/links` and `artifacts/jumplists` say the same: the independent `lnkinfo` read is made for the first 400 links of a run, a legacy `.LOG` goes in as `log1`, and what the container checks cover.

`lnk_jumplist` no longer lets a DestList make it work out of proportion to the file. Each DestList entry was matched to the streams by a pass over every stream, and to the file by a pass over every fragment of the DestList, with no look at the clock: 100,000 entries and 20,000 streams in a 14 MB file took 238 s although `max_seconds` was 10. Entries are now matched by name and by bisection, the clock is looked at while they are, and the same file takes 1.1 s. A DestList is read to its first 50,000 entries (`jumplist` and `lnk_jumplist` alike; Windows keeps a few thousand at most): what follows is named by the stream offset it starts at and the number of bytes, as a problem that makes the status partial, and a stream whose entry may be among those not read is not called an orphan. A 66 MB DestList of 580,000 entries, which was held whole (873 MB, 18.7 s), is read at 149 MB in 1.7 s.

`registry_recover` holds its independent reader to a ceiling. A damaged 8 KB hive made libregf's `regfexport` take 5.5 GB and print 5 to 8 GB before it crashed; it is now started with an address-space limit (a gigabyte and four times the hive) and stopped when it prints more than 64 MiB and 64 times the hive, and the same hive ends in 0.8 s at 76 MB. When it is stopped, fails or does not finish in time, `independent_reader.regfexport.why_not_finished` says which, its counts are not compared, and the status is partial (it was complete before). The clock is also looked at while the hive, the logs and the recovered copy are hashed: a hash the time limit stopped is not made, the receipt names it under `not_hashed`, and the status is partial; a 3.9 GiB hive with `max_seconds` 3 took 6.5 s, now 3.0 s. A recovered copy that was placed when the tool was stopped by a signal before its receipt was written is removed, and the status says so. When the time limit is reached before a log entry that could be applied was found, the reason says that, not that the log holds none. The count of regfexport's `Key path:` and `Value:` lines counted a line again when a read of the pipe ended inside it and the carried bytes held it whole, so on a damaged hive the two readers could disagree by a count that depended on how the output was cut; each line is now counted once.
