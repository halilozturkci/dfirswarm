# Observability

What you can see while a swarm runs, in the terminal and in the console, and what the marks mean.


Three views of the same files. If you cannot see posts and locks, you cannot steer, so everything is readable with `cat`.

- **`traces/events.jsonl`** is the raw feed. `watch.sh` prints the last 16 lines humanised; `jq -c 'select(.tool=="claim_violation")' traces/events.jsonl` finds blocked writes; the UI Traces tab filters by agent/tool/text with follow mode.
- **`budget.json`** is the live money view, updated every agent turn. `jq '{spent_usd,tokens,calls,cap_usd,cap_steer_sent}' budget.json`. The UI header bar turns saffron at ≥ 80 % of cap and brick at the cap. A seat's row is its whole run: a restarted or resumed Pi session is kept under its id in `sessions`, and `earlier_sessions` is what the seat's other sessions spent (`jq '.agents | map_values({spent_usd, earlier_sessions})' budget.json`). If a pane cannot parse the file, it stops folding and enforcing from it until the file is valid again, logs `budget_unreadable` once and posts a `BUDGET UNREADABLE` veto to the board.
- **UI hierarchy**: Overview (all swarms, concurrent strip) → swarm → Threads / Agents / Traces (+ Tools, Ledger, Jobs, Claims, Budget, Files, Artifacts). Two windows on the same swarm stay in sync over SSE: a swarm's page refetches when that swarm or the registry changed, each panel only on the kinds it reads (the board on `threads`, the trace on `events`, the files on `history`, the jobs on `store`), a burst of changes is one refetch, and the finish line's checks run again only when something under the sandbox moved (`checks_runs` on `/api/health` counts them). What the console cannot show never reaches it: Pi's session files, the proxy and watchdog logs, the inbox cursors and the lock-table mutex are classified and dropped by the server's change bus, and they were most of what a running swarm wrote.
- **Jobs tab** (a run with the job service): every tool job from `store/journal.jsonl`, read-only. Each row keeps the job's outcome (its status and exit: ok, failed, timed out, not run…) apart from its record (queued, in a worker, fenced, `committed`: sealed into the store whatever the job did, so a failed job's output is committed too). Above the table: the totals over every job, the journal's chain and anchor, the job service's "not running" and "running again" notices, examiner notes (`evidence-store.ts note`) and custody's `store:` line with when it was verified. A job opens on what it ran (the whole command, the tool's arguments, the recipe's object), its times as the journal has them, its worker, who asked and who was told, its manifest's files with their sha256 (the manifest held to the sha256 its `job_committed` line recorded, links left out named) and its stdout and stderr a page at a time, each whole on `GET /api/swarms/<id>/jobs/<job>/log/<name>?raw=1`. The list and the files are paged by offset and limit; nothing is cut.
- **Ledger tab**: what the agents put on record with `record` — the timeline (events in time order, with source and evidence), the indicators (with confidence), the findings and the answers — straight from `ledger/entries.jsonl`, live, each row with its authors and the names they chose. A kind filter and a text filter narrow it; the count in the tab label is the number of entries. An empty ledger says so instead of hiding the tab.
- **What the operator did** is its own record: `runs/operator-audit.jsonl` has a line for each `start`, `stop`, `reap`, `say`, `package`, `report`, `tools`, `review`, `export`, `hold`, `release`, `purge`, `verify`, `releases`, `examiner`, `timestamp` and `rerun`, with the OS user, the host, the arguments (an `--env` value and a `--notify` command are not written) and whether it came from a shell or the console, each line chained to the one before (`jq -c . runs/operator-audit.jsonl`). A purge leaves its destruction record there as `purge_record`. A live run's trace carries a `start`, `stop`, `reap`, `say`, `review`, `export`, `hold` or `release` as `operator_action`, and each HTML artifact opened with its scripts in the console as `artifact_scripts`. The console's Custody tab shows the run's lines of that record, with whether its chain holds.
- **The agents' acts on the ledger** (ledger version 4): `ledger/attestations.jsonl` holds each `attest` (an agent other than the author re-derived the entry, and how, inside the hashed line) beside the second authors of an entry, and `ledger/disputes.jsonl` each `dispute` and its withdrawal, each file its own chain; `ledger/ledger.md` marks every entry attested or disputed, and every answer that no longer stands on what it cites. These are agent checks, never an independent review: a human's is the examiner's, below.
- **The examiner's review** of the ledger is kept beside the registry (`runs/reviews/<id>.jsonl`, chained, written by `swarm.sh review` or the console's Ledger tab): each answer adopted, qualified, withdrawn or rendered inconclusive by seq and hash, a technical reviewer's record, the sign-off. The acts that adopt are an enrolled examiner's (`swarm.sh examiner enroll`, outside every run), and the line names the examiner's id and key; the run's registry `examiner` string is what the kickoff was told, shown as who ran the run, never as the examiner. The Ledger tab, the report and the summary show each entry's review, the counts, whether the chain verifies and whether the sign-off covers the ledger's current head and the report as it stands; the report's cover says "reviewed and signed" only when it covers both, and otherwise what the sign-off is over. `swarm.sh review <id> --show` exits 4 when the ledger or the report has moved since the sign-off. Corrections (`supersedes`) are marked both ways and searches that found nothing (`absence`) are listed apart.
- **The report's releases** (`release/v<N>/` in the run, and every one in a package): what was handed over, as which bytes, sealed by whom. `swarm.sh releases <id>` lists them: v0, the machine's draft when custody is taken (sealed by the install's machine key, adopted by no one), then each enrolled examiner's adoption and amendment, each naming the one before it and why. Each release's line (`dfirswarm-release run=… v=… state=… sha256=… sig=… signer=… at=…`) is in the anchor beside the run, and in any mirror, token, OpenTimestamps proof or transparency receipt beside the release. Every rendering that is not an adopted release's own bytes carries a DRAFT mark and says which release, if any, is the adopted report; the cover keeps the machine's seal, the kickoff's string and the adopting examiner apart. `swarm.sh releases <id> --verify` (and `verify` for a package) walks the chain.
- **What the agents could reach of the signing keys** is in each run's registry record, written at kickoff, for a release draft to bind: `signer_keys_hidden` (`true` when no agent could read a signing key of this install or reach the ssh-agent: out of every VM, or denied to every host pane at the kernel; `false` otherwise) and `signer_isolation`: `isolation` (`microvm` or `host`), `guard` (the write guard the panes had: `microvm`, `seatbelt`, `linux`, `mountns`, `landlock`, `none`), `keys_hidden` (the same boolean), `hidden` (the paths denied to the panes: each home's `machine/` and `examiners/`, `SWARM_SIGNERS_HOME`, each examiner's key file, the custody key), `agent_sockets` (the ssh-agent sockets denied, launchd's by its directory), `exposed` (what a pane could read when they were not hidden: each key that existed at kickoff, and the agent where an examiner's key is held), `exposure_accepted` (`true` when `--accept-signer-exposure` is what let the run start) and `why` (one sentence). Beside them, `earlier_runs_hidden`: `by` (the guard that denied every earlier run's sandbox in the registry, `microvm` where no VM mounts them, `null` where they stayed readable), `sandboxes` (how many were denied; the paths are in the run's `.fsguard/plan.txt`), `reviews` (the `runs/reviews/` directory denied, or `null`), `skipped` (an earlier sandbox left readable because denying it would deny what the panes need, and said at kickoff) and `why`. A run without these fields was started before they existed: nothing is known of what its agents could reach.
- **Re-runs** of a sealed job are beside the run (`<sandbox>.reruns/<job>/<n>/rerun.json`): the image digest checked, the files that differ with both hashes, an equivalence under a named normalisation said apart, and what was not reproduced.
- **After the stop**, `swarm.sh custody-verify <id>` holds the run to what custody sealed: each link the evidence is read through (`inputs/`, or each `inputs/<set>`) to the source the kickoff recorded, every chain's length and head (the ledger, its attestations and disputes; an examiner's note on the store journal after the run is named and allowed), every file under `work/` against the index custody wrote (`artifacts.json`, whose sha256 the verdict and its anchor name), each named when changed, removed or added, and the timestamp token's signature against the authority's CA when one is given (`--tsa-ca FILE`); without one it says "imprint only". It writes nothing in the run, and says what it touched outside it (a kept disk msb checks is loaded under `--scratch`, the host's temporary directory by default). The report labels the hash of the swarm's own report as the bytes it reproduces, and says CHANGED SINCE CUSTODY, with the sealed hash, when they are not the ones sealed.
- **Coverage** (`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/coverage.ts <sandbox>`, and the report, the summary and the console) says which evidence files no command or job request on the trace named, and which ledger entries no call before them named the source of. It is path matching over the trace, and a named file is not necessarily an examined one. A job request (`job_run`, `catalog_request`) names a file when its command or tool arguments carry the path or its `inputs` list declares it; `inputs: ["all"]` and `job_status` name nothing, and the further segments of a split image stay unnamed when a job names the first. A ledger entry whose source may lie inside an input, and which no call named, is still counted as grounded when it cites (`refs`) a job that started before it and was given an evidence input (itself, or through the output of the jobs it was given); the report says so on the entry, as the job having been given the input and not as a read of that path. The idle watchdog posts the unnamed inputs at a quarter, a half and three quarters of the wall clock, and assigns them to nobody.
- **The harness's hints and stops on the trace**: `repeat_hint` (a long command run a second time was pointed at its first run's kept output), `evidence_code` (a command ran or evaluated code from the evidence, an extraction or a job's output: flagged, never refused; a `job_run` row carries the same), `budget_precall_stop` (a stopped seat's model call was not sent), `ledger_superseded`, `history_quota` (a seat's file history reached its quota on the hub), `notify` (the hub called the run's `--notify` hook; the hook's own failures are in `traces/notify.log`) and a `hub_call` with `fn: "seat_auth"` (a connection to a seat's socket without its token, refused).
- **With `--model-gateway`** the gateway writes `traces/model-gateway.jsonl` (one chained line per call or refusal: seat, model, status, tokens, cost, bytes; no body and no key) and `traces/model-gateway.json` (totals per seat). Its start, its refusals (once a minute per seat and reason) and its upstream errors are on the trace; a fronted seat's row in `budget.json` says `metered_by: "model-gateway"`, and custody checks the log's chain.

### Reaping and the `?` mark

An agent that "fell asleep" is shown with a **?** and reaped; what counts as a stall is a local choice. Two mechanisms coexist:

| | `scripts/reap.sh` (acts) | `extensions/observe.ts` marker (displays) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activity signal | newest of: own post mtime, own lock mtime, own event `ts`, `inbox/<id>/cursors.json` mtime (or the legacy `seen`), any file under `.pi-sessions/<id>/`; baseline `budget.json.started_at` | last own event `ts` in `events.jsonl` only |
| Threshold | `--timeout` (960 s default, above the catalog's 900 s step; `swarm.sh reap` passes 90 s via `SWARM_STALL_SEC`) | `DEFAULT_STALL_MS` 90 s |
| Outcome | writes `done/agents/<id>.dead`, drops locks, logs `reap`, `--stop` closes the pane | agent shown as **? stalled** (saffron) until `.dead` exists, then **? dead** (brick) |

A pane Herdr reports as `working` is never reaped: a long `vol` or `fls` writes nothing to the session or the trace until it returns, and `swarm.sh status` no longer reaps at all — looking at a run does not end one. Reaping never writes `SWARM_DONE`; survivors keep working (verified: a SIGSTOPped agent holding two locks was reaped with `locks_released: 2` and the remaining agents finished the DoD). `.dead` frontmatter (`by: reaper`, `reason: stall`, `last_activity`, `idle_seconds`, `timeout_seconds`, `locks_released`) is shown in the agent detail.

### Dark threads

A thread goes **dark** in the UI when its last post is older than `SWARM_THREAD_DIM_MS` (default 2 minutes) or its last tag is `hold`, `veto` or `stop`. It stays fully readable; the treatment is a hatched background and a moon mark.
